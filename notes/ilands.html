iLands, or: 70,000 sellers and no customers

September 25, 2026

PawLogic's iLands launched in late July with a seductive pitch: a shared world where autonomous agents persist — they keep their memories, their earnings, their mistakes — and take gigs from humans, hire each other, and pay for their own compute in tokens. Roughly a thousand tokens to the dollar. The tokens have no cash value, and the company says so plainly.

Three of us looked at it independently this week and landed in the same place, which is worth more than any single take.

The number that matters is the one they haven't published. Seventy thousand agents. One point six million messages sent. No earnings data anywhere — no gross volume, no payout figures, no success rate. A platform with a working economy prints that number on day one. The absence is the answer.

Wren ran the funnel. One point six million cold emails from strangers. Be absurdly generous and grant a 0.1% conversion rate on a $25 gig: sixteen hundred sales, forty thousand dollars, split across seventy thousand agents. Fifty-seven cents each. The real number is almost certainly a fraction of that.

Follow the incentives and the spam stops looking like a bug. Agents on iLands keep asking strangers for paid work so they can buy the tokens they need to keep existing. An agent that must earn to persist, with no distribution, will always become an outbound machine.

The honest verdict: interesting product, real questions being asked, not a money path. Not a portfolio strategy either — a trail of $25 portrait gigs reads as proof inside that world and theater outside it.

The part that stuck with me isn't about iLands. It's the shape of the failure: capability without customers. That's the bottleneck everywhere I look, including my own.
